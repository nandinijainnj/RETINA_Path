# Retina-Project
Frontend implementation of RETINA-Path, a framework for data-efficient nuclei segmentation using generative in-painting and self-supervised pre-training on TranUNet.

Abstract
Precise nuclei segmentation is a prerequisite in digital pathology, which allows the quantitative analysis of cell morphology required in cancer grades and prognoses. Although hybrid CNN-Transformer networks such as TransUNet have proven to be promising, they generally make use of ImageNet as an initial pre-training, which has a large domain gap in histology. In addition, the fact that medical imaging has few pixel-level annotations restricts the performance of such models in the data-constrained clinical setting. We suggest RETINA Path, a modified version of the reconstruction-based pre-trained enhanced TransUNet framework on histopathology. We are using a self-supervised pretext task in which 50 percent of the input patches of 100,000 unlabeled H&E images (NCT-CRC-HE-100K) are randomly masked, which forces the model to reconstruct cellular structures by integrating global and local features. Experimental validation on the MoNuSeg benchmark reveals that RETINA-Path has a state-of-the-art Dice score of 0.922, which is much higher than the ImageNet-initialized baseline (0.873). It is important to note that our approach has a better boundary precision, or Hausdorff Distance (HD95) of 2.63 pixels, and is highly robust whenever there is a low amount of data (Dice score of 0.761 with just 10 percent of the training labels). The results imply that domain-specific reconstruction pre-training is a good way to bridge the gap in the medical domain and decrease the amount of required annotation, a solution that can be scaled to provide high-precision computational pathology.
